\section{Error Analysis}

The confusion matrices localize the main errors in visually similar categories. Table~\ref{tab:error-analysis} reports error counts derived from the class recalls in the 1,000-example-per-class test set.

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \scriptsize
    \caption{Major error groups across all models. Counts are errors within the indicated true-class subset.}
    \label{tab:error-analysis}
    \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{2.6cm}p{3.5cm}X@{}}
        \toprule
        Error group & Errors: Linear / MLP / CNN / GRU / ViT & Cause and improvement direction \\
        \midrule
        Shirt & 767 / 446 / 192 / 390 / 432 out of 1,000 & Shirt shares outlines and textures with T-shirt/top, Pullover, and Coat. For ViT, the confusion matrix shows 165 Shirts predicted as T-shirt/top, 119 as Coat, and 108 as Pullover. Use class-aware hard-example sampling, stronger local features, and inspection of ambiguous annotations. \\
        Pullover + Coat & 600 / 446 / 215 / 407 / 278 out of 2,000 & Similar sleeves, torso shapes, and low grayscale resolution. ViT confuses Pullover with Coat 64 times and Coat with Pullover 71 times. Test deeper convolutional features or targeted augmentation that preserves garment identity. \\
        Sandal + Sneaker + Ankle boot & 541 / 206 / 77 / 126 / 197 out of 3,000 & Pose and sole shape overlap. ViT confuses Sandal with Sneaker 81 times and Ankle boot with Sneaker 43 times. Analyze high-confidence footwear mistakes and test targeted spatial features. \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

For GRU, the most prominent single confusion is Shirt predicted as T-shirt/top (196 cases); Pullover and Coat are also frequently exchanged. For ViT, Shirt is the weakest class, with 432 errors out of 1,000; its mistakes are spread mainly across T-shirt/top, Pullover, and Coat. CNN has the fewest errors in the listed clothing and footwear groups. These patterns are consistent with overlap among low-resolution garment silhouettes; architecture changes alone may not resolve every ambiguous case.
